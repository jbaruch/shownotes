## Areas for Improvement (Dimension 14)

Time management is the dominant failure and it is structural, not incidental. Two of six promised features — local models and guardrails — were cut, and slide 11's fully-built guardrails diagram was never narrated. The final third is visibly compressed: "10 minutes to finish," "30 seconds," "let's we're running out of time," "No, we're out of time." `shortchanged`, severity `warning` — and it is this speaker's top recurring antipattern.

Filler density is the second issue: 377 uh/um across ~46 minutes, 8.2 per minute, 5.0% of every word spoken. `hiccup-words`, severity `warning`. Co-presentation doubles the exposure — two speakers each filling their own handoff gaps.

Third, the talk repeatedly narrates its own machinery: the recording dying ("I hope you record guys because you cannot rely on this one" / "you up the recording again?"), the hotspot ("let's see if my internet works"), the SD card, the wrong window, and the forgotten voting mechanism. `going-meta`, severity `info` individually but `warning` in aggregate.

Fourth, the five stage diagrams set labels at roughly 8–10pt equivalent inside a panel with large unused canvas — "Select Tool," "LLM Formats Response," "Updated Context" are unreadable past the front rows, and the speaker narrates them, so they are payload on an unreadable slide rather than a deliberate reward layer. `ant-fonts`, severity `warning`. The same diagrams are also stylistically off-brand: clean tool-generated flowcharts with emoji glyphs sitting inside a hand-lettered comic frame.

Finally, the closing under-delivers the format's own showpiece. The vote is the designed climax and it was run one-sided — the winning framework was never counted, so the payoff of a 46-minute bake-off landed as "I guess launch 4J." Polling both sides costs ten seconds and is the difference between a verdict and a shrug. Underuse note: `guess-first` fired once (the P-in-GPT question, which the room answered and which earned a t-shirt) and worked; in a talk whose whole shape is a comparison, asking the room to predict which framework wins each stage before the reveal is free and unused.
