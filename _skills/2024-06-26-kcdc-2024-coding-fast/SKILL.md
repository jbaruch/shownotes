DIMENSION 1 (OPENING). Delayed self-introduction, hard. Title (1-2), a memorial card for Kahneman dead three months (3), the book (4), then the bat-and-ball riddle and its solution (5-6) BEFORE the bio at 7 and shownotes at 8. The first thing asked of the room is a poll ("how many of you heard about Professor Kahneman -- not enough, a lot, not enough") and the second is a shouted answer. Room-state is read and weaponised: "afternoon Friday last day, we almost made it" and "you don't want to employ your System 2 because it's Friday, you are tired, it's been a long conference -- I get it" -- the audience's actual fatigue is not small talk here, it is the experimental condition for everything that follows.

DIMENSION 2 (STRUCTURE). Problem-diagnosis-solution over six sections with a single throughline (mental fuel): Kahneman theory -> live depletion -> "you run on System 1" -> "OK code" as the cost -> fuel-saving techniques -> DPE -> faster feedback. Consistently inductive: the riddle runs before System 1/2 is named (9), the puzzle-plus-Stroop runs before "you have mental fuel" (17), the triangle sandwich runs before "the problem" is named (31). Three clean concrete-before-abstract sequences. No agenda slide, no progress marker, no section numbering anywhere in 64 slides -- boundaries are signalled only by background-colour change.

DIMENSION 3 (HUMOR). Situational and self-implicating rather than gag-based. "Who am I to ask you to actually work for me"; "that was just evil of me" as the reveal of the triangle trap; "(DUH)" printed under the fatigue-research quote; "I have no idea what this study is about -- it all looks like this"; "so basically task batching -- don't do Java code, counting triangles, Java code... so didn't land well" (an explicit on-mic acknowledgement that a joke missed). The Chernobyl "NOT GREAT, NOT TERRIBLE" still (40) is the deck's single best beat because it is simultaneously the joke and the definition of "OK code".

DIMENSION 4 (AUDIENCE INTERACTION). Full list in structured_data. Density is roughly one every four minutes and it never lets up: the last two polls land at ~36 minutes.

DIMENSION 5 (TRANSITIONS). ## Areas for Improvement (Dimension 14)

1. `floodmarks` (warning). A four-element footer plus a Gradle elephant runs on all 57 content slides in a fixed light colour, and on three slides it overprints content: slide 19's chart axis labels, slide 63's "Early-Bird ticket for $249", and worst, slide 27's third card -- "It takes 25 minutes to return focus to a task after interruption". That is the number the talk's entire commercial payoff is computed from ("your build is 25 minutes"), and it is the one line the branding sits on top of.
2. `ant-fonts` (warning). Slides 19, 27, 48 and 63 all carry text nobody can read, and the speaker says so out loud on 19: "I have no idea what this study is about -- it all looks like this." Three of these are screenshots scaled until they bleed off the frame; the cropping is a production defect, not a `second-look` reward layer.
3. `bullet-riddled-corpse` (info). Slides 49/50/51 are three consecutive text-only three-bullet slides read as a list, with the speaker conceding "most of them I'll just mention and this is not the place to talk about them." One slide would have done the same work.
4. No `bookends` (info, no antipattern id). The bat-and-ball riddle is the most memorable artifact the talk produces and it is never re-invoked. A ten-second return -- "so, the ball costs five cents, and your build takes twenty-five minutes" -- was available and unused.
5. No `new-bliss` (info, no antipattern id). The future-state ("Run on System 2 all-day") lands on slide 58, four slides BEFORE the call to action, and the last thing the room sees is a conference discount. The emotional close is traded for a commercial one.
6. No `context-keeper` / `breadcrumbs` (info, underuse). Six sections, 64 slides, 44 minutes, and zero positional cues -- no agenda, no progress marker, no numbering. Section boundaries are carried entirely by background colour.
7. Unrealised `live-demo` (info, underuse). The talk's only concrete performance claim -- 2 minutes to 6 seconds via parallel builds -- is asserted and explicitly not shown, in a delivery that finished on time with five minutes spare. A thirty-second recorded terminal capture would have converted the argument's weakest link into its strongest.
8. No spoil-recovery instrument on the opening experiment (info, underuse). Someone shouted the correct answer three minutes in ("ah okay, you ruined it") and the recovery was to narrate the counterfactual. The talk's flagship proof is one loud audience member away from becoming an anecdote, every single delivery.

POSITIVE, against Section 15's oldest recurring issue: with only SEVEN days between the Devoxx Poland and KCDC deliveries -- the corpus's highest-risk interval for the "conference branding oversight on reused decks" pattern documented since 2018 -- every footer across all 64 rendered pages reads #KCDC, with no #DEVOXXPL survivor anywhere. Also, slide 63's "Register by August 18" deadline was still live on 2024-06-26; the corpus's expired-promo complaint attaches to a later delivery of this same slide, not to this one.
