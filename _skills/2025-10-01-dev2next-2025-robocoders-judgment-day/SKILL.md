This is a distinct delivery with its own recording (IcWW5Ln5CYQ, 10,496 words / 66m35s) that shares neither transcript nor deck with Devoxx Poland (u6dWLYQHpkg, 18,123 words). Everything below comes from the dev2next recording alone. No deck was analyzable: the PDF on file is Devoxx Poland's (created 2025-06-11, '#DevoxxPL' footer, two contenders, June model lineup, no IIC slide), so Dimension 13 and the deck half of Dimension 8 are unjudged.

DIM 1 (opening). Not a cold open on content but a cold open on CONTINUITY. The first words are a callback to the duo's earlier dev2next session: 'Thank you all for coming and thank you some of you for coming back. That's very surprising after you experienced previously that you wanted to come back.' Then the self-deprecating escalation — 'if you think that what happened in the previous talk was unrehearsed dumpster fire — well, it was — was nothing compared to what's going to happen now.' Within ninety seconds both bios land flat and fast ('this is Bar Sudagorski' / 'This is Victor Gamov ... from TuxCare' / 'Victor from Confluent'), which REFUTES delayed-self-introduction for this delivery. The real hook is structural: they immediately run the FINISHED application and show the physical smart bulb, then delete the whole codebase on stage. That is concrete-before-abstract executed as theater.

DIM 2 (structure). Three parallel tracks, not three acts. Baruch drives VS Code + Copilot under his Intent Integrity Chain; Viktor drives Windsurf on Sonnet 4.5 under what HE calls PDD (prompt-driven development, PRD-first, Grok-researched); Junie is withheld until minute ~40 and revealed as the third contender running the same requirements in YOLO mode. Note the role reversal against the stored entry: in this delivery PDD is VIKTOR's method name and the Intent Integrity Chain is Baruch's. The through-line is a race whose outcome the recording never reaches.

DIM 4 (audience). Ten room-production events in 66 minutes: an opening vibe-coding show of hands that lands thin ('All right, few people. I'm curious why ... why people not enjoying the vibe coding'), 'How many of you heard about the context window?', 'anyone using IntelliJ products like JetBrains products?', 'how many of you read the amazing book called Head First Java? Couple of hands', a guess prompt ('who do you think won the chair?'), a recruited spectator in a red t-shirt for the webcam colour test, a second appeal for lighter clothing when Viktor's black shirt defeats the detector, 'please tap the button', an unsolicited mid-talk question, and a four-t-shirt bounty announced explicitly to buy more of them ('Let's make it more interesting. I have four t-shirts to give for non-stop questions and none of them are stupid'). The bounty pays off — a legacy-codebase question arrives later and is rewarded on the spot ('That's definitely a t-shirt'). nodding-room is refuted outright.

DIM 5 (transitions). The transition device IS the pattern: 'Okay. While you do that, why wouldn't we switch and talk a little bit about what the difference between vibe coding and AI assistant engineering'; 'as a next step I will pass it microphone to Bar'; 'while we on this subject I want to talk about another engine'; 'All right. I switch to me while you battle with that.' Every conceptual segment exists to fill an agent's compile time. This is expansion-joints as the load-bearing architecture rather than a safety valve.

DIM 3 / 10 (humor, references). Far drier than the Devoxx Poland sibling and with a completely different reference set. The single sustained bit is the Head First Java chapter-one parable (procedural dev ships fast, OO dev wins long) deployed at ~minute 62 precisely when both demos are stuck, with the punchline 'who do you think won the chair? ... the boss's secretary obviously' — then turned immediately into the talk's thesis: 'jokes aside we are now at exactly this point.' Other beats are situational: 'if it compiles ship it' (twice), 'no databases, no Redis, for god forbid', 'I'm a millennial so obviously there should be emojis there', 'burn my ten mile bucks a month ... don't ask me anything. Do your thing. If I will see you doing something stupid, I will tell you.' Zero movie references, zero memes verbalized, no monkey metaphor, no Karpathy.

DIM 7 (verbal signatures). 'Intent integrity chain' 11 times (across six ASR spellings), 'Context 7' 23 times, 'stop gate' 4, 'YOLO' 4, 'it compiles, ship it' 2. Naming is contested on stage as a running bit: Baruch's 'vibe coding but with helpful commentary on the way' versus Viktor's 'we like to call it AI assistant engineering because ... we're Java champions, we have like some age and mileage.'

DIM 8 (slide-to-speech). Judgeable only from the spoken side, and the spoken side says this is a near-deckless delivery. Two slide moments surface in 66 minutes — a session-locator ('this is where you are right now') and the Intent Integrity Chain diagram, introduced with an apology ('this is a very um kind of that's the slides that you get for this presentation. Sorry about that'). Everything else is screen-share: IDEs, terminals, Docker Desktop's MCP toolkit, the Context7 website. Whatever deck existed carried near-zero structural load.

DIM 9 (persuasion). Mechanism-register throughout, with one strong inoculation beat up front: 'a lot of people are frustrated about vibe coding because all the downsides of LLMs — the hallucinations, the drive to please, you're absolutely right, and the tests that instead of validating behavior validate existing code and always pass — and we all know that because we tried and we were burned with it.' Credibility is earned by artifact rather than assertion: the Shelly API documentation in Context7 'was added one month ago by someone that you know.' Notably there is not a single number, benchmark, percentage or citation anywhere in the recorded 66 minutes.

DIM 11 (technical delivery). The strongest dimension. Both methods are shown as executable artifacts, not slogans: Copilot instruction files with a Context7 documentation-authority rule and explicit stop conditions, phase gates that halt for human approval, Gherkin feature files generated into src/resources, tests carrying Javadoc traceability back to the original prompt, and the chmod-read-only trick that locks the model out of its own tests. Viktor's counterpart shows PRD -> plan -> tasks, Windsurf rules files, per-diff accept/reject, and a screenshot-to-agent debugging loop, later escalated to a Puppeteer MCP driving a real browser.

DIM 12 (pacing). 157 WPM across 66.6 minutes. Compression is visible twice and both times it costs the argument: Baruch skips the plan-review step his own methodology mandates ('We are time constrained but in the real world you read the plan, right?') and Viktor abandons the full build ('I tell it we don't — we're running out of time. So just do MVP').
