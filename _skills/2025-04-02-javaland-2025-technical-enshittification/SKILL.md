## Rhetoric Notes (Dimensions 1-13)

CORRECTION TO PRIOR ANALYSIS. The brief's 'comic-book, meme-dense' characterization is half right and half wrong, and the artifacts settle it. The comic-book aesthetic is confirmed on the rendered pages: halftone-dot backgrounds, a white comic panel with a heavy offset black shadow rotated a few degrees off-axis, hand-lettered Bangers display type on all 88 content slides, and speech-bubble section dividers. But this is NOT an illustrated deck and not a meme-dense one. There are no AI-generated or custom illustrations at all. The comic treatment is a TEMPLATE wrapped around real-world screenshots, and the deck's dominant atom -- 40 of 89 slides -- is a punchy editorial title over a screenshot receipt: a news article, an arXiv abstract, a vendor page, a Wikipedia diff, a market-share chart. Memes are 5 slides, not 8. The prior 'image_only_slide_count: 25' is wrong by an order of magnitude: exactly 2 slides show no authored words (44, a Scrubs mask-pull reaction; 70, an embedded reclaim.ai screencast). Every other slide carries a title, a chart, or baked-in headline text.

DIM 1 (Opening). Cold open, no preamble, no agenda: 'Thank you, hello and welcome. So little time, so much [shit] to cover. Let's get started.' Profanity inside ten words, then straight into legitimising the coinage -- enshittification winning word of the year by a huge margin, then Doctorow's socio-economic definition, then the narrowing move to a purely technical reading scoped to the room. Bio is deferred to slide 6 (~2:20 in). The shownotes slide (7) is announced as 'the most important slide of this talk' and is stated to be live and populated before the talk begins.

DIM 2 (Structure). Three acts as three nested numbered catalogues: 5 symptom stations, 8 cause stations, 7 fix stations, with the count restarting per act, hinged on an explicit turn slide (67, 'WE CAN FIX SOME OF IT' over the Serenity Prayer). Sonos is the master story -- planted at slide 19 with an explicit promise, recalled at 43 and 45, then supplying the evidence for five of the eight cause stations. One case carries the entire diagnostic act.

DIM 3 (Humor). Sustained comic register, roughly a laugh line every 60-90 seconds, and the humor is load-bearing rather than decorative. The best beats are structural: 'the reason why it's 1.2 is because you cannot give zero stars'; the CrowdStrike airport story landing on Windows 3.1; 'Facebook used to say move fast and break things -- well, they don't move fast anymore, so I'm not sure what's their excuse now'; the deadpan 'AI generates tons of bad code much faster than humans, which is pretty awesome.'

DIM 4 (Interaction). Eight interaction moments; three drew audible responses. Two of them are textbook guess-first: 'just scream if you think you know -- how many times people are checking email during the day' (room: 7, 30, 80; answer: 77; he works the miss -- 'who said seven? I love your optimism'), immediately followed by 'getting back to what you have done before takes how long?' (room: 20, 10, 25; answer: 25). A calibration poll on Sonos usage draws a thin show of hands and he adjusts by defining the product. The rest are rhetorical.

DIM 5 (Transitions). Hard cuts to numbered speech-bubble dividers, verbally bridged: 'so why all this happening, let's look why'; 'remember Sonos? let's look why'; 'all right so now we have five minutes to fix it'. Twenty dividers act as intermezzi between evidence runs.

DIM 6 (Closing). Three-part close on slide 88, a shownotes callback closing the opening loop, then a thanks slide with handles across four networks. The summary is read aloud verbatim.

DIM 7 (Verbal). 'You had one job' recurs as a title formula; 'nom-noms RAM' runs across three consecutive slides; 'obviously' (22x) and 'right?' (21x) are the connective tissue. Filler is heavy: 98 'um' plus 56 'uh' in 5,498 words. Note a transcript caveat -- the ASR cannot render 'enshittification' and produces 'Ancien ification', 'intific' and 'justification' throughout.

DIM 8 (Slide-to-speech). The slides carry more than the narration does, but the excess is evidentiary, not a reward layer. He narrates the number the red box points at (30,000 open Meta bugs; 40% of AI-generated code vulnerable; 65.2/46.3/31.1; 704,102 malicious packages; 52 minutes of code time; 5,000 vs 15,000 characters of Windows feature diff). The surrounding article body is unreadable from the room and is not meant to be read -- it functions as attestation that a real source exists. That is why this deck is neither second-look (no unnarrated buried payoff; the detail is narrated, not withheld) nor ant-fonts (the room never needed to read it; the title plus the spoken number carry the point). Exception: slide 39, where the boxed figure -- Acrobat's 900MB disk requirement against Sumatra's 16MB -- is the punchline and the narration drops the number, so the room got half a joke.

DIM 9 (Persuasion). Data-first, with the numbers spoken rather than left on the slide, and consistent inoculation against the obvious objection ('don't get me wrong, full self driving now is absolutely amazing'; 'DevOps team is a very bad idea, but DevOps as an idea is definitely great'). Stance is peer-who-got-burned, not expert: the LastPass confession is the clearest instance.

DIM 10 (Culture). Stalin's 'quantity has a quality of its own' inverted for software; the Serenity Prayer as the act hinge; Scrubs, The Office 'they're the same picture', Pikachu; Ozempic as a page-weight gag. Earned rather than decorative -- each one is the argument, not a break from it.

DIM 11 (Technical delivery). No live demo. One embedded screencast (slide 70, reclaim.ai) narrated live. Complexity is handled by concrete artifact first, generalization second, with red boxes doing the pointing that a laser pointer would otherwise do.

DIM 12 (Pacing). 89 slides in 43:29 = 2.05 slides/min against a mode-a budget of 1.4 and a stated catalogue-pace target of 20-30 sec/slide. Word rate is a comfortable ~126 wpm, so the pressure is in the slide count, not the speech. The symptom act runs long and the fix act pays for it.

DIM 13 (Design). Two fonts total. Backgrounds rotate across roughly ten halftone hues with no semantic mapping -- rotation is rhythmic, not coded; consecutive slides simply never repeat a colour. Persistent footer triplet (@jbaruch / #Javaland / speaking.jbaru.ch) in hand-lettered type plus a TuxCare mark bottom-left on slides 7-89; small enough to sit under the floodmarks threshold. Section dividers are a distinct visual type: giant numbered phrase in a speech bubble on flat halftone, no panel, no footer competition.

## Areas for Improvement (Dimension 14)

Act-3 compression [shortchanged, severity: warning]. The title sells two halves and the runtime delivers 79/21. 'Now we have five minutes to fix it' is announced at 79% of the transcript, and the last three fix stations get roughly 30 seconds each -- headline plus four sub-bullets, no example, no evidence. This is the speaker's most-recurring antipattern (113 of 191 scored talks) and it reappears here in its self-inflicted form: nothing was cut externally, the symptom catalogue simply over-ran. The symptom act is the compressible half -- five or six of the thirty-odd receipt slides could go without weakening the case.

Filler density [hiccup-words, severity: info]. 154 um/uh in 5,498 words (2.8%), concentrated where he chains examples without landing pauses. The word rate (126 wpm) leaves room for the pauses; they are just not being taken.

Retrieval clustered, close read aloud [nodding-room, severity: info]. The two guess-first moments are the strongest three minutes in the talk and they are the only three. Nothing is asked of the room in the first 25 minutes or the last 12, and the close reads a fully-populated three-bullet summary aloud at the exact moment recall would have paid most. Asking the room to name the three takeaways costs 20 seconds and converts the best-remembered slide from recognition to retrieval.

Cultural exposure [alienating-artifact, severity: info]. Uncensored profanity in the first ten words and a Stalin propaganda poster with massed soldiers, at a German venue. He contextualizes the Stalin slide himself, which is the right instinct; the opening profanity gets no such cover.

One broken beat (no antipattern). Slide 39's Sumatra-vs-Acrobat gag depends on a figure the narration drops; the room saw a red box around a number nobody read out.
