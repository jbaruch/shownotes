## Rhetoric Notes (Dimensions 1-13)

Dimension 1 (Opening). The talk opens on a question to the room in its first fifteen seconds - whether the talk will have AI - and takes a show of hands before anything else happens. Deck slides 1 and 2 carry only the title and subtitle inside comic panels: no agenda, no housekeeping, no biography. The hook is also a plant that the talk pays off much later.

Dimension 2 (Narrative structure). One throughline runs end to end. Act one establishes System 1 and System 2 experientially, act two shows that System 2 runs on a finite fuel the owner cannot measure and that the failure surfaces as invisible 'okay' code, and act three answers with context engineering across three named lanes. The pivot is explicit and pivot-shaped rather than gradual. The solution half is a clean triad - personal, organizational, technological - and the speaker numbers the third lane aloud. Four of the five sparkline elements are present; only the closing future-state vision is missing.

Dimension 3 (Humour). Humour is structural rather than decorative. The Nobel Prize bit compresses the entire behavioural-economics tension into one deadpan reversal; the back-to-office section lands its shared-context argument and then undercuts it with tax incentives; the agent-failure sequence escalates through three real failures. The transcript records only one explicit laughter marker (the return to Java puzzlers after the triangle count), which understates the room, but the audience-supplied guesses throughout show a live room.

Dimension 4 (Audience interaction). This is the talk's dominant rhetorical engine. Roughly twenty-eight distinct moments put the room on record: show-of-hands polls, shouted number guesses, a sustained collaborative code review of the Java puzzler, and a Stroop test the audience performs aloud. Crucially the guesses are wrong in the useful direction, and the speaker exploits each gap rather than moving on. Polls are framed positively ('who heard about caffeine nap') and answered with teaching rather than shaming.

Dimension 5 (Transitions). Transitions are mostly verbal and mostly explicit - 'back to Java puzzlers', 'okay, organizational context engineering', 'and technological context engineering, that's the third part'. In the solution half they are also visual: three identical speech-bubble divider slides on rotating halftone colours. The problem half has no such markers and relies entirely on the spoken bridge.

Dimension 6 (Closing). A recap slide of six items, then a thanks slide carrying the handle, the topic invitation and a QR code, with a second QR for the speaker's own London conference. The close is a to-do list plus a contact ask; it does not lift to a future-state image, which is the one structural gap in an otherwise complete persuasive shape.

Dimension 7 (Verbal signatures). Kahneman's own hedge, 'if you are like most of the people', is reused as a way to let the room be wrong without embarrassment. 'It's all about context' is the thesis refrain. 'Come on' and 'do an effort, really count' keep a poll alive rather than rescue it.

Dimension 8 (Slide-to-speech). The deck consistently carries more than the narration does, and deliberately so. Research slides show real paper abstracts at sub-room scale under a room-legible red callout; the agent-failure slide shows three full chat transcripts of which only fragments are read; the claw-ecosystem landscape shows several dozen logos in a single glance gag. The recoverable destination is stated in the opening and repeated in the footer of every slide. Against that, the solution half hands the narration over to bullet lists and the recap slide is spoken almost verbatim.

Dimension 9 (Persuasion). Authority is borrowed and then checked: Kahneman, Csikszentmihalyi, Gloria Mark, Gladwell and Mednick are all named, and the cellular-metabolism paper is used to put a chemical mechanism under Kahneman's model rather than to decorate it. Objections are voiced by the speaker before the room can raise them, including one that directly contradicts his own earlier claim. Personal experience carries the AI section: the agent failures are first-person, and the four-times eval lift is his own measurement.

Dimension 10 (Cultural references). Barney explains a neuroscience paper, Futurama Fry carries the same suspicion twice, Chernobyl's 'not great, not terrible' names okay code, and the two Spider-Men close the human/machine equivalence. Each does argumentative work rather than filling space.

Dimension 11 (Technical delivery). Three Java puzzlers are used as System 2 load rather than as teaching material, and the speaker says so out loud. Code is shown as full static listings with no highlighting or scrolling, the weakest part of the visual handling. No live demo.

Dimension 12 (Pacing). The diagnosis half takes roughly two thirds of the transcript and the solution half one third, after an out-loud time check. The second half is dense: three lanes, sleep science, a calendar tool covered twice, the back-to-office study, the agent story and the context-engineering payoff. The Java-puzzler and triangle interludes function as genuine attention resets.

Dimension 13 (Slide design). A single comic-book metaphor governs the deck: halftone dot fields in nineteen distinct colours, black-outlined white comic panels with deliberately crooked edges, seven-point starbursts for declarations, speech bubbles for section names, and a Bangers/Sniglet pairing with Fira Code reserved for listings. Builds are used heavily - 117 visibility actions across 37 slides, 30 build lists, zero animation-behaviour elements and zero slide transitions - so the motion vocabulary is appear/hide rather than movement. Progressive reveal is implemented as consecutive duplicate slides rather than native animation. ## Areas for Improvement (Dimension 14)

Pacing is the clearest issue. The speaker announces the remaining time out loud at the pivot and then compresses the entire solution argument into that window, so the half the audience is meant to act on gets a third of the runtime while the diagnosis gets two thirds (shortchanged, weak severity - the transcript shows the budget being named and the second half running under it, but without delivery video the visible-strain half of the criterion cannot be confirmed).

The solution half also changes register in a way the first half never does. Where the diagnosis teaches by making the room fail at something, the prescriptions arrive as five consecutive titled bullet lists and a six-item recap that is spoken almost verbatim off the slide (bullet-riddled-corpse, moderate severity). The technique that carried the first half - make them guess, then show them - is available here and unused: nothing asks the room to predict which intervention actually restores fuel before sleep is revealed as the only one that does.

Several beats step outside the talk to comment on its own construction: reading a speaker note aloud, telling the room that having read the book ruins the experience, announcing the slide count after the thank-you (going-meta, moderate severity). Each lands as a laugh and is moved past quickly, so the cost is small, but three in one talk is a habit rather than an accident.

Three smaller, talk-local items: the reclaim.ai material is split across two distant visits and the second has to re-establish what the tool was; the close ends on a to-do list where the talk has earned a picture of the engineer who still has fuel at five o'clock; and the deck carries a slide reading 'quite room' and 'reserves taped' plus one whose footer still shows another talk's hashtag.
