## Rhetoric Notes (Dimensions 1-13)

Dimension 1. The talk opens on a bold claim rather than an agenda: after roughly thirty seconds of welcome and an audible equipment check, the speaker announces a conspiracy theory and asserts that devops is a plot by ops people to make developers work harder, then promises proof. Page 1 carries the title only, page 2 stages a published conspiracy-severity chart so the speaker can locate his own claim near the grounded end of it, and the bio does not arrive until page 3. The hook buys the room before the credentials are spent.

Dimension 2. The structure is a courtroom brief wrapped in a joke. One throughline, the conspiracy, is asserted, evidenced across six exhibits, and then explicitly resolved mid-talk with a verdict that concedes the charge and forgives it. That resolution immediately reopens tension (the accused will not come to the table) and the second half diagnoses why through terminology, then proposes DPE. A summary page restates the whole thing as an evolution. The arc is complete and every section serves it.

Dimension 3. Humor is dense and almost entirely load-bearing. The comedy engine is the conspiracy frame itself: each exhibit is played as evidence in a case the speaker knows is absurd, which lets him make a sharp argument while disclaiming it. The best beats are structural rather than verbal - the ten-frame walk across the book cover exists only to make one absence land, and the sad-to-happy aside about the direction of animated reaction images is a genuine observational joke that also carries the pivot.

Dimension 4. Interaction is present but thin for a room this size. There is one real commitment moment, where the room is asked whether working from home is more or less productive, answers audibly, and has its answer used rather than overridden. The rest is rhetorical address with cover built in: the audience is told to say yes about the book even if they have not read it, and pointed at the link. Roughly five interaction moments across twenty-eight minutes, plus a short Q&A with one question.

Dimension 5. Transitions are mostly verbal and explicit, and several are deliberately comic rather than smooth. The speaker announces his own rage, defers it, then returns to it by name several minutes later. The deck supports transitions with colour rather than with a system: register shifts track background changes, red for indignation, orange for declarations, purple for pivots, blue for the solution half. Only two pages function as true section dividers, and they do not share a design.

Dimension 6. The close is a summary followed by a resource list. Six bullets restate the argument in full sentences, four asks follow, and then the social and shownotes page. There is no callback to the conspiracy frame and no future-state image; the talk ends lower than it opened.

Dimension 7. The verbal signature is a hedging-then-committing rhythm: a claim is asserted flatly, then softened with a stacked qualifier, then reasserted. Recurring markers include the is-not-a-thing construction, an appended right as a consent check, kind of sorta more or less as a deliberate imprecision marker, and anyway as the standard re-entry after a digression. Two profanities are bleeped in the recording.

Dimension 8. Slides and speech are tightly coupled but not redundant. For most of the talk the deck carries the exhibit and the speaker carries the reading of it, which is why the wordless full-bleed pages work. The relationship inverts at the end: the summary and action pages contain complete sentences that the speaker then reads, and the audience is given nothing to do but read ahead. The published deck also carries more than the room can consume - several pages hold detail that only rewards the later visit the shownotes footer advertises.

Dimension 9. Persuasion is evidence-first throughout. Claims are introduced as artifacts before they are named: a book cover, a results page, a forum answer, two salary distributions, a job count and a certification page. Authority is borrowed carefully, with a named industry CTO quoted with attribution and vendor survey figures sourced on the card. The speaker names his own commercial interest at the point where the data would otherwise read as a pitch.

Dimension 10. The cultural layer is the deck's spine. A Scooby-Doo unmasking template is reused twice on two different targets, a streaming series poster is repurposed to make an org-structure pun literal, a Sisyphus photograph carries the ops workload, an Austin Powers air-quote still carries the collaboration engineer gag, and the ape-to-desk evolution silhouette carries the closing succession claim. A then-current internet meme about a certain empire is used as a frequency comparison, which dates the talk precisely.

Dimension 11. There is no demo and no code anywhere in the 73 pages. Complexity is handled by substitution rather than simplification: instead of explaining what a platform engineer does, the speaker stages a chat assistant defining it and then agrees with the definition. The technical content is the thinnest part of the talk by design; the argument is definitional and organizational.

Dimension 12. Pacing is front-loaded. The conspiracy proof consumes roughly the first third with ten pages spent on a single book cover, which is affordable because the payoff is one line. The middle third moves fast through terminology. The DPE section, which is the part the talk exists to sell, gets the least room and leans hardest on vendor cards to do its arguing. The summary is rushed and read.

Dimension 13. The visual system is a single sustained comic-book vocabulary: halftone dot grounds in a rotating palette, one hand-lettered display face used for every headline and the footer, black-outlined speech bubbles with tails, an irregular hand-inked starburst reserved exclusively for the speaker's own declarations, and white panels skewed a few degrees off axis with hand-drawn drop shadows. A white elephant mascot and a four-element footer sit on 67 of 73 pages. The system holds across every authored page; it breaks only where third-party artifacts are imported straight - four vendor statistic cards bring their own typography, and nine screenshots sit inside the frame unstyled. Fourteen pages carry a speech bubble, three carry the starburst, and 26 pages are memes or meme-plus-caption. Notably, the published PDF collapses at least one build: page 54 prints two states of the same three-circle diagram on top of each other, with labels overlapping.

## Areas for Improvement (Dimension 14)

The close is the weakest structural element. Page 71 is a six-bullet summary in complete sentences that the speaker reads almost verbatim, and page 72 is a four-item action list read the same way - `bullet-riddled-corpse` in a deck that otherwise refuses bullets, and the one place the audience is handed text to read ahead. Nothing in twenty-eight minutes asks the room to reassemble the argument for itself, and the talk ends on the to-do list with no future-state image and no callback to the conspiracy frame that opened it.

Three bare screenshots carry no room-legible layer at all: the forum answer, the search results page, and the six-tile job grid. Their type is far below back-of-room size and no headline carries the point for the people who cannot read them - `ant-fonts`, and the fix is one comic headline per page, which the deck already does well elsewhere. The four imported vendor statistic cards bring a third typeface into an otherwise two-family system and one of them collides with the comic headline placed over it - `fontaholic`, minor but visible.

The talk goes meta three times: an equipment check in the opening seconds, a recording-and-upload logistics detour, and an unresolved question about whether time remains for questions - `going-meta`. Each is brief, but the timing one lands right before the Q&A and reads as unpreparedness rather than as the deliberate close it could be.

The persistent four-element footer and mascot on 67 of 73 pages is `floodmarks` at the moderate tier. It earns part of its keep as the shownotes destination, but it does not need to be on the wordless full-bleed pages, where it is the only thing competing with the image.

Page 2 puts a published conspiracy-severity chart on screen as a gag, which projects Holocaust denial and an explicitly labelled antisemitic threshold for the sake of one laugh line - `alienating-artifact` at the weak tier. The joke works without the chart being legible; cropping to the grounded-in-reality end would keep the beat and drop the risk. Finally, audience interaction is underused for a talk with this much energy: one real commitment moment in twenty-eight minutes, when the exhibit chain is built to invite prediction at almost every step.
