## Rhetoric Notes (Dimensions 1-13)

ARTIFACT CHECK FIRST. The launch brief's framing survived contact with the artifacts, but the prior DB record did not. The deck is 91 pages (pdfinfo), slide_source is pdf only, and there is no pptx, so there is no second artifact to disagree. Critically, this PDF carries a real vector text layer: pdftotext returns authored slide text for 84 of 91 pages. That refutes the old record's image_only_slide_count of 18 outright. Exactly ONE slide in the deck shows no words at all - slide 45, the Scrubs 'sad doctor removes his mask' reaction shot. The other six pages with no vector text (2, 31, 71, 74, 77, 86) are all full-bleed rasters with dense text baked INTO the image: the Macquarie Dictionary Word-of-the-Year donut chart, the Stalin poster reading 'QUANTITY HAS A QUALITY OF ITS OWN', a Reclaim.ai calendar UI, the Qodo and Google Books pages, and a custom comic illustration whose speech bubble reads 'Security is a dev problem now'. Judged from rendered pages at 110 and 150 dpi, this is a text-forward deck, not a wordless one.

D1/D2 OPENING AND STRUCTURE. The talk opens on a definition, not on the speaker: title, then the Word-of-the-Year chart, then Doctorow's definition in a starburst, then his socio-economic model as a three-arrow flow, then the reframe ('a purely technical, albeit reductionist, view'). Only on slide 6 does the speaker name himself - a clean delayed-self-introduction. The macro arc is three acts the speaker marks aloud: a catalogue of five failure categories (9-46), eight root causes (47-67), seven remedy categories (68-89). Inside each act the spine is a numbered listicle announced by an identical speech-bubble divider; twenty of those dividers carry the whole navigational load. The Sonos app-relaunch disaster is the connective tissue - planted at 19-20, resolved from 46, and returning seven times in all, each return anchored to the same Ars Technica headline card with a different paragraph red-boxed. That is a master-story executed with a visual anchor, and it is what the prior analysis mislabelled as unifying-visual-theme.

D3/D4 HUMOUR AND INTERACTION. The register is dry escalation rather than meme density: 4 meme slides in 91 (0.044/slide) against this speaker's own mode (a) baseline of 0.24. The laughs come from the situations - a hallway of blue screens, Southwest surviving CrowdStrike because Windows 3.1 has no agent, a 1.2 star rating 'because you cannot give zero stars'. Nine audience-address episodes, four of which draw audible answers. Two of those are textbook guess-first: the room commits a number ('15. Half an hour. Next day.') before the 25-minute refocus reveal, and the reveal explicitly credits the guesses, then the move repeats on the build-time question and lands 'twenty-five minutes exactly'.

D5/D6 TRANSITIONS AND CLOSE. Transitions are hard cuts softened verbally ('now we're getting to the second part of our talk and this is why'). The close is the talk's weakest structural moment and its clearest departure: two slides only. Slide 90 merges summary and CTA into one bulleted panel, slide 91 is thanks plus socials. This speaker's documented mode (a) default is a three-part close, and the two sibling mode (a) talks in the reparsed cohort both carry three-part-close, coda and new-bliss. None of the three is present here.

D7 VERBAL SIGNATURES. Tag-question 'right?' 21 times, 'obviously' 13, 'absolutely' 12 as an intensifier, plus the escalating 'nom-noms ram' run and the 'you had one job' construction reused across two slides. Filler load is heavy: ~255 tokens in 6,601 words.

D8/D13 SLIDES. The deck is a complete comic-book system - halftone backgrounds rotating through a ten-name palette with almost no adjacent repeats, a tilted white panel with a heavy hand-drawn border and offset shadow, hand-lettered condensed all-caps titles that never change colour because the panel isolates them, a persistent three-element footer on 84 slides, and a TuxCare penguin on all 91. Roughly 46 slides embed a source screenshot at native body-copy size with a red box around the one sentence being narrated. This is the deck's most interesting and least catalogued behaviour: at 150 dpi on slide 55 the Ars Technica headline is room-legible and the body paragraphs are not, yet the speaker paraphrases those paragraphs aloud, so the audience never needed to read them. That makes it neither second-look (the fine layer is narrated and holds no unnarrated payoff) nor cleanly ant-fonts (nothing was withheld) - see catalog_feedback.

D9/D11 PERSUASION. The load-bearing claim is defended in four registers, which is why walk-around fires and golden-rule does not: a number (52 minutes of code time a day), a mechanism (a 25-minute refocus cost turns a 5-minute build into a 25-minute build), a human consequence (grounded airlines, leaked vaults, his own travel day), and a forward implication. Objections are pre-empted repeatedly ('there are good reasons for it not to be 8 hours...', 'why do we care about the size of software in 2025...', 'disclaimer, I have Cybertruck'). No demo; all technical content is delivered through cited evidence.

D10/D12 REFERENCES AND PACING. Cultural material is functional rather than decorative: Stalin's cannon-fodder line as the bloat thesis, Ozempic for page weight, Pikachu for Chrome RAM, the Serenity Prayer at the act-3 pivot. ## Areas for Improvement (Dimension 14)

Compression is the headline issue and it is self-inflicted. The speaker flags it in the first two minutes ('I only have 91 slides to go over 45 minutes so it will be close') and then narrates the consequence on air: 'we have five, ten minutes to fix everything - let's fix everything in 10 minutes.' Twenty-two remedy slides run in roughly ten minutes and Q&A is dropped. The deck HAS the cut lines - seven independently droppable remedy sub-sections - and none was cut; everything was compressed instead. [shortchanged, severity: hard_limit]

Filler density is high enough to be audible: ~255 tokens in 6,601 words, about 5.7 per minute, rising in the rushed final act. [hiccup-words, severity: warning]

Six separate moments talk about the talk rather than the topic - an equipment fumble left in the opening, a reference to the recording in progress, the slide-count aside, the ten-minutes announcement, 'I did a very stupid proxy metric' undercutting his own Windows evidence, and the closing question about whether there is time for questions. The proxy-metric line is the costliest: it invites the room to discount a data point he chose to present. [going-meta, severity: warning]

Roughly 46 slides carry source text far below any readability floor. Nothing was withheld from the room, so the harm is small - but the red annotation boxes are themselves an admission that the surrounding text cannot be read. [ant-fonts, severity: info]

Profanity on slides and the Stalin cannon-fodder gag in front of a German audience are flagged under the entry's 'even mild instances' directive, though both sit inside this speaker's declared mode (a) register. [alienating-artifact, severity: info]

UNDERUSE, no antipattern ID. The close is two slides. This speaker's own mode (a) default is a three-part close, and the two reparsed mode (a) siblings both carry three-part-close plus coda plus new-bliss. All three are missing here, and the talk ends on a bulleted recap rather than on any elevated image.
