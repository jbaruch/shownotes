## Rhetoric Notes (Dimensions 1-13)

ARTIFACT CHECK FIRST - the brief's framing is only half right. The launch brief calls this a leadership-audience talk in the profile's mode (d) 'business-case advocacy', characterised by professional register, minimal memes and funnel-reframing structure. The artifacts confirm exactly one of those three and contradict the rest. The deck is not professionally registered at all: it is the speaker's standard comic-book halftone template - hand-lettered Bangers titles in tilted white panels with heavy black outlines, speech bubbles, eight starburst slides, a rotating halftone colour register, and a Gradle elephant watermark on every page. It is visually indistinguishable from his mode (a) developer polemics. What DOES shift is joke density and vocabulary, and only that.

SOURCE DIVERGENCE (verified, must be recorded). The pptx named in the DB ('Engineering succcess.pptx') has 86 slides. The published Google Drive PDF has 75 pages, byte-identical to a local export, and four sibling files named 'Engineering succcess static*.pptx' each have exactly 75 slides. Diffing shape text and shape counts shows why: the 86-slide master carries four progressive-reveal build sequences as separate frames - the autonomy/mastery/purpose table (34-37, shape counts 3/5/6/7), Gloria Mark's three attention statistics one per frame (42-44), the Agile-vs-DPE comparison table row by row (62-65), and the SPACE breakdown (75-78) - and each 'static' slide equals the FINAL frame of its build (static 34 has 7 shapes; master 37 has 7). The narration follows the build frames exactly ('we are checking our emails 77 times a day... how about our attention span... 47 seconds... and the most terrible... 25 minutes'), so the delivered experience had 86 visual states and the published artifact has 75. All indices below are keyed to the published 75-page PDF for internal consistency; delivered frame count is recorded separately. No SmartArt anywhere - no diagrams/ parts in either package; the circular implementation diagram (71) and the federal/state/local funnel (70) are shape groups.

EXTRACTION NOTE. 27 of 86 master slides report text_extraction_confidence 'low' with background_type 'image'. OCR produced nothing on any of them, because the illustrations are BACKGROUND FILLS and the OCR pass only reads PICTURE blobs - the only picture shape on most of those slides is the 0.003-ratio elephant watermark. Every density, meme and image-only judgement below therefore comes from rendered pages, not from shape text. The prior analysis's image_only_slide_count of 12 is corrected to 5.

1. OPENING. Cold open on a self-deprecating room joke ('I'm going to blame the low attendance on the organizers labeling the room incorrectly - that's my story and I stick to it'), then a rhetorical inventory of what managers think productivity means (fancy office, latest Macs, better coffee, noise-cancelling earphones as a gift) demolished with 'but it's actually not very good.' Then eight straight slides of industry-collapse data. Failure framing, not a poll or a story.

2. NARRATIVE. Problem-diagnosis-solution, running as a Duarte sparkline. Baseline ('welcome to 2023'), gap ('Happiness or productivity? Pick two'), oscillating middle (four pillars, each a developer complaint answered by a DPE tenet; then two objections answered), and a new-bliss close on 'Developer Joy Team'. The four pillar sections are structurally identical - tenet map with the relevant tenets highlighted, pillar title with a comic character, a starburst developer quote, then survey data - which makes the middle navigable.

3. HUMOUR. Continuous but dry and situational. No performed bits. Best beats: 'I abused the Switzerland top-level domain here, very proud of that'; 'the only game I play is Civilization so I have no idea what's going on there'; the carrot-and-stick escalation 'it works on donkeys, it doesn't even work on kids - you know that'; and the anti-sell 'that was my product pitch, thank you very much.' Profanity is present but mild: 'annoys the hell out of everybody', 'DPE the hell out of our entire Enterprise', 'this schmuck just will go no', '25 minutes suck', 'stupid people doing stupid [things]'. Five instances in 47 minutes.

4. AUDIENCE INTERACTION. Thin, and this is the talk's real weakness. One genuine show-of-hands attempt ('who realized that they do it 77 times a day - anyone?') that draws nothing and is self-answered in two seconds. Otherwise the room's voice is delegated entirely to slide characters. Two live Q&A questions and one Zoom-chat invitation at the very end.

5. TRANSITIONS. Visual and repetitive by design. 'It's harsh out there' repeats as a section header three times, accumulating weight. The tenet map recurs five times as the structural hinge. Verbal bridges are plain and explicit ('so I want to go back to those tenets', 'next question after we figured out that someone has to do it is - okay, how do we measure').

6. CLOSING. Three-part: value summary, action summary, CTA with QR, then handles. Both summary slides are five-bullet lists read out almost verbatim - the weakest 90 seconds of the talk. He finished early and offered 'questions, or we can just be the first at the break.'

7. VERBAL SIGNATURES. 'It's harsh out there', 'and it's terrible', 'well, you know what', 'in the end of the day', 'obviously' (14x), 'kind of' (11x), 'you know' (23x). Rhetorical self-Q&A is his default engine: 'what does it mean? It means...', 'so what does work? ...'.

8. SLIDE-TO-SPEECH. Slides carry more than the narration in two directions. Downward: the borrowed research charts hold citations and sample sizes he never reads. Upward: the comic panels carry the punchline while he supplies the setup. He never reads a slide except at the close. Roughly 1.6 published pages per minute (1.8 delivered frames per minute).

9. PERSUASION. This is the talk's strongest dimension and the one worth reporting to the orchestrator. All four evidence registers are present and the central claim is defended in every one of them - see walk-around above. The golden-rule antipattern, which an executive audience is the natural place to find, is absent: no archetype is missing, and neither Q&A question attacked a gap. The first question ('I just care about deadlines, money') was a human-consequence challenge he had already answered, and the second (prove it locally) had been pre-answered by the federal/state/local frame.

10. CULTURE. Full Metal Jacket drill sergeant, Iron Man's hologram, Dilbert's pointy-haired boss (twice), Lego minifigures, Johnny Cash. Books do the heavy lifting: Pink's Drive, Noer's Healing the Wounds, Mark's Attention Span, Goldratt's The Goal, Forsgren's SPACE paper. Every reference is load-bearing.

11. TECHNICAL DELIVERY. No demo. Complexity handled by analogy - big ball of mud, boiling the frog, the smartphone that gets slow after two years, federal/state/local government - and by a repeated concrete anchor: the ten-minute build that is really a twenty-five-minute build, which returns three times and lands the 'fixed it to seven minutes, still 25' punchline.

12. PACING. Even. 47 minutes plus ~6 of Q&A against a 60-minute slot; no visible time panic and no rushing. One unplanned 40-second detour when the Zoom screen share and camera failed mid-section.

13. SLIDE DESIGN. Comic-book halftone dominant, with a foreign layer of roughly 13 unrestyled third-party slides (Gradle TechValidate, Leadership IQ, BizReport, ACM Queue) in their own palettes and typefaces. Two fonts of his own (Bangers 79 uses, Sniglet 25). Multiplication-sign bullets. Four-element footer plus elephant watermark on slides 19-75, absent from 1-18. Eight starbursts, three formal speech bubbles. Hand-drawn red annotation is the deck's one consistent authored-on-top convention, used six times.

## Areas for Improvement (Dimension 14)

The dominant issue is `nodding-room` [CONTEXTUAL, severity: warning]. Forty-seven minutes of well-built teaching with exactly one production moment - a show of hands that drew nothing and was self-answered in two seconds - and a close that reads two five-bullet summary slides aloud. Every callback restates rather than asks. This is the highest-leverage fix available: the ten-minutes-versus-twenty-five-minutes anchor is perfectly shaped for a retrieval prompt at the Agile-vs-DPE comparison ('so what was our build actually costing us?'), and the federal/state/local frame could be handed to the room to apply instead of demonstrated.

`dual-headed-monster` [RECURRING, severity: warning] - already at 8.5% of the corpus and increasing. The hybrid was run with no operator; the screen share and camera dropped mid-section and he found out from the room. The remote audience was addressed once, in the final 20 seconds. This is a logistics fix, not a rhetoric one.

`bullet-riddled-corpse` [RECURRING, severity: info] - only 9 of 75 slides, but the two that matter are the closing summaries, read out verbatim. `borrowed-shoes` [RECURRING, severity: info] - 13 slides of unadapted vendor and research collateral, each in a foreign palette, with the fixed white footer sitting on white backgrounds at near-zero contrast. `hiccup-words` [RECURRING, severity: info] - 1.9 um/uh per minute plus heavy 'you know' / 'kind of'.

Underuse: `guess-first` and `retrieval-beat` are both structurally available here and both go unused; the SPACE framework and the manager-versus-IC divergence are natural predict-then-reveal beats. Notably absent: `shortchanged`, his single most recurring antipattern at 59% of the corpus, does not appear - he finished with time to spare.
