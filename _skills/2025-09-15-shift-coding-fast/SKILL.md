## Rhetoric Notes (Dimensions 1-13)

ARTIFACT CHECK AGAINST THE BRIEF. The brief's characterisation holds: this is a 70-page comic-book halftone deck (mode l, Behavioral-science-bridge, Coding Fast and Slow family), Infobip Shift Zadar, 15 Sep 2025, delivered solo in English (yt-dlp auto-captions are .en; no non-English passage anywhere). THE STROOP TEST — THE HEADLINE FINDING. The wording DID change from the siblings, and the outcome did not. This delivery's instruction, verbatim and complete: 'And that's the only thing that I want you to do. Now we're going into the puzzle. Now, what I want you to do is shout really loud the color of the letters. Let's do it.' The siblings' strategy cue — 'with System 2' — is GONE. That half of the contamination was repaired. What survives is the target cue ('the color of the letters'), which is structurally inseparable from the instruction, plus a NEW contaminant unique to this delivery: 'Now we're going into the puzzle' announces that a trap is coming before the first slide advances.

The room's response, verbatim: 'Yellow, orange, red, blue, green.' The five stimulus slides are p14 RED in yellow, p15 GREEN in amber, p16 YELLOW in red, p17 BROWN in blue, p18 ORANGE in green. That is five ink colours out of five, in order, with no audible hesitation, no laughter, no wrong answer. The interference did not occur — for the fifth observed delivery running.

The speaker's verdict, delivered immediately after a room that just got it right: 'Good job. Now, what you wanted to shout is actually what is written on the slides.' He then compounds it at 12:24: 'the reason why it was even harder for you is because your system 2 was pretty involved with the puzzle, right? You depleted your mental energy.' Both sentences assert an effect the room demonstrably did not exhibit, and 'even harder' claims a difficulty that produced zero observable errors. An audience member intervenes at 12:12 ('The first one later on might bring back'), and the speaker's own live answer names the root cause exactly as the siblings did: 'Yes, especially in the first one, because then you kind of already know that you need to engage system two. That's exactly the thing.' The room was primed, the speaker knows it, and the conclusion is asserted on the counterfactual anyway.

BAT-AND-BALL. The pre-muzzle IS present and did its job: 'If you shouted 5 cents, you're probably cheating. You know about this problem before.' But this delivery's bat-and-ball verdict is unfalsifiable by construction, and for a different reason than the Stroop. The speaker states outright that he did not observe the result — 'Okay. Excellent. So I cannot hear you because of the noise' — and then hedges the verdict into a counterfactual: 'if you said 10 cents OR THOUGHT 10 cents, you are a normal person.' No room response can disconfirm that. Same failure family, different mechanism: the Stroop asserts against contrary evidence, the bat-and-ball asserts where evidence was structurally excluded.

THE MEMORY QUIZ. Absent entirely. There is no five-digit-number slide anywhere in the 70-page deck, no 'remember this number' setup in the transcript, and no cash-in. The word 'remember' appears three times and never in a memory-task sense. This is not an abandoned promise — the promise was never made. If the siblings set it up and dropped it, this delivery repaired it by removal.

The talk's central mechanism is therefore evidenced once and asserted once, and only the asserted half gets narrated as proof.

## Areas for Improvement (Dimension 14)

Six issues, in severity order.

1. The Stroop demonstration is asserted, not observed [going-meta adjacent but really an evidence-integrity failure; severity hard_limit, no clean antipattern ID]. The room answered 5 of 5 ink colours correctly and the speaker said 'Good job' and then told them they had wanted to say the written word. Dropping 'with System 2' from the instruction was the right repair and it was not enough; the surviving fix is to stop naming the target before the first slide (show one warm-up slide, take the room's answer, only then say what the task was) or to retire the conclusion and keep the exercise as a shared joke. Five deliveries of asserting a result the room refuted is a pattern, not an accident.

2. shortchanged [severity hard_limit; RECURRING — 113 of 191 corpus talks, the speaker's top antipattern]. 30 of 35 minutes on diagnosis, then 21 slides in 5 minutes. The fix is structural, not verbal: the framework act needs a cut line, because the exercises are the part he will never cut.

3. going-meta [severity warning; RECURRING — 29 percent of corpus, trend increasing]. Six-plus timing, camera and commercial intrusions, worst at 24:26 where a sentence about uninterrupted flow is itself interrupted for camera management.

4. bullet-riddled-corpse [severity warning; CONTEXTUAL]. Confined to p51-p69, and a direct consequence of issue 2 — the compressed act had no time to be anything but read aloud.

5. hiccup-words [severity info; RECURRING — 24 percent of corpus, trend increasing]. 2.9 percent filler in the first five minutes against 0.5 percent in the last five.

6. Underuse and unforced errors, no antipattern ID. Bookends never fires: the bat-and-ball and the Stroop are never called back at the close, which is the cheapest available upgrade to a talk that opens on two memorable exercises. The Q&A bait planted at 07:17 with a gift attached is never harvested. And peer-review is refuted by two typos on projected slides — 'Loose-loose' for lose-lose (p34) and 'LAURATE' for laureate (p3) — plus overlapping title text on the p50 divider.
