## Areas for Improvement (Dimension 14)

The headline defect is the unbuilt promise. The deck opens 'JUDGMENT DAY', 'AI CODING AGENTS FACE OFF' and 'Contenders' and the delivery never states a criterion, never polls, never tallies and never declares a winner [abstract-attorney, warning]. Building the competitive frame into the artifacts and then dropping it verbally costs the talk its only structural tension; two minutes of criterion-setting at slide 3 and a 60-second tally before 'THANKS!' would have converted the whole 90 minutes into an argument. Time management repeated the family's chronic failure [shortchanged, hard_limit]: the third contender never finished, the closing slides were sprinted in about a minute, and the deck's own payoff was replaced with 'cur completed demo. Let's pretend that it actually managed.' Kiro consumed roughly twenty minutes and delivered no narrative return, functioning largely as cover for the other laptop [dead-demo, warning]. Filler is heavy: 294 'uh' plus 228 'um' in 90 minutes, about 5.8 per minute, plus 'and what's not' as a tic [hiccup-words, warning]. Meta-commentary is pervasive and mostly avoidable -- sound checks, 'I'm trying to figure out if I started Claude in the right directory', 'ah your PowerPoint. You love PowerPoint so much. It hijacks stuff' [going-meta, warning]. The opening Die Hard reference landed with roughly two hands and the response was to scold the room ('That's appalling. It's like two hands.'); it is also the deck's bookend, so the frame the talk closes on is one most of the room never held [alienating-artifact, info]. Screenshots on 22, 25 and 28 are unreadable past row three, and slide 15's chain labels are 12pt inside 1.8-inch chevrons [ant-fonts, info]. Evidence is defended almost entirely in the mechanism register -- workflows, step lists, the chain diagram -- with no numbers, no cost, and no human consequence; the one precision question in the room ('is there any summary of how many tokens used?') had to come from the floor and was answered 'there is no easy way to collect all this' [golden-rule, info]. Two proofreading misses reached the screen: 'Formulize' for 'Formalize' on slides 9 and 14, and one slide branded with the wrong talk's hashtag (slide 22, #INTENTINTEGRITYCHAIN).
