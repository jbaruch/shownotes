## Rhetoric Notes (Dimensions 1-13)

CORRECTION FIRST - the DB pointed at the wrong deck. The talk entry's `pptx_path` was `DevOps Vision/2023/Ignite Karaoke.pptx`, a 70-slide deck for an entirely different format. The deck actually delivered is `DevOps Vision/2023/DevOps without Authority static.pptx` (111 slides), which matches the published Google Drive PDF exactly (111 pages). A 115-slide `DevOps without Authority.pptx` also exists; animation-node counts are identical in both (2), so the 4-slide delta is editing, not build expansion. Every finding below is re-derived from the correct artifacts.

SECOND CORRECTION - this is not a wordless deck. 109 of 111 slides carry real shape text (titles, bullets, footers); only slides 27 and 29 have neither shape text nor OCR text, and both are in fact full-bleed images (a Lenin propaganda poster and a Michael Scott reaction still). The reason OCR returned text on exactly one slide is that the illustration layer is delivered as PowerPoint background picture fills (`<p:bg>` on 109 slides), which the shape walk and the tesseract pass over PICTURE blobs both miss. Prior `image_only_slide_count` of 32 is an overcount; the rendered pages show 23 genuinely wordless slides.

THE FRAMEWORK IMPORT - and how it differs from the 2019 sibling. This talk does hang its entire structure on an imported model, but the import is handled differently from the sibling described in the brief. The model is the Cohen-Bradford Influence Without Authority (IWA) six-stage cycle: assume all are potential allies / clarify your goals and priorities / diagnose the world of the other person / identify relevant currencies, theirs and yours / dealing with relationships / influence through give and take. Attribution is explicit but external to the artwork: slide 32 is the book cover (Allan R. Cohen | David L. Bradford), the narration credits it ('what we're going to look at is called the coin [Cohen] Bradford model... it's based on their book by the same name and you know where to find the link to this book, speaking.jbaru.ch'), and in Q&A he says outright 'obviously I selected the Bradford Cohen model because it's so easy and this wow effect... there are tons of books that are exactly that, how to lead from behind, leadership without authority - there are a bunch of them, they're all the same'.

The diagram itself is NOT a screenshot with a copyright line left intact. Rendered at 190dpi it is a clean redrawn vector - blue arrows, pink ellipses, sans-serif labels - sitting on the deck's own halftone-dot texture with the deck's own Bangers footer. No credit line, no book chrome, no screenshot artefacts. So: the concept is imported and credited verbally, the artifact is re-rendered into the deck's own visual system.

THE WAYFINDING IS AUTHORED, NOT INHERITED. The recurrence device is the speaker's own addition, not something that came with the framework. Across six recurrences (34, 40, 45, 75, 80, 83) the diagram is pixel-identical to its base (33) - a byte-level diff touches only 0.6-0.7% of pixels - and the sole change is a red ellipse ring the speaker drew as a PowerPoint OVAL shape over the current node. Global shape counts corroborate: exactly 7 OVAL shapes in the whole deck, six of them these rings plus one ringing a face in a comic panel. Two further contrasts with the sibling: there is no dimming (mean luma is 226.0-226.8 on every recurrence, flat to within a rounding error), and the marking is two-state rather than three - completed stages look identical to upcoming ones. And unlike the sibling, the navigational patterns here are not carried by the import alone: `bookends` runs on the speaker's own business-card motif and `intermezzi` on his own Darwin/Evolutionary-Pressure slides, both independent of the framework.

A SECOND FRAMEWORK, THIS ONE HALF-AUTHORED. Slides 61-72 traverse a matrix of Autonomy / Mastery / Purpose / FEAR against Dev / Ops / QA / Security / Product. The first three columns are Daniel Pink's Drive, credited by book cover at 59 and hand-sketched at 60; the fourth column is the speaker's own addition, stated as such ('a simple matrix of autonomy, mastery, purpose with an addition of fear'). The artifact is entirely his: red and blue marker on a spotlit worksheet, paired with a black right-hand panel carrying the current cell label and a role badge on a lanyard. Worth recording precisely, because it looks like a you-are-here device and is not one: pixel comparison across all twelve frames shows the grid content is identical and only the framing zoom shifts. No cell is ever highlighted. The traversal is announced verbally by the panel title while the matrix sits behind as a fixed backdrop - `context-keeper` behaviour without any `traveling-highlights` behaviour, the exact inverse of how the Cohen-Bradford diagram is handled twenty slides earlier.

## Areas for Improvement (Dimension 14)

1. `going-meta` (severity: warning). The opening minute is spent on equipment and housekeeping - a blank slide, a hunt for the clicker, an admission of nerves, and a mid-talk recording check that drafts an attendee as 'my video monitor'. The Alex hook is strong and lands about a minute later than it should.

2. `floodmarks` (severity: info). The persistent footer collides with content on full-bleed slides. On slide 16 the footer runs across the 'Low' performer row that the deck's own red highlight box is emphasising; same on 15 and 47. The deck's branding is degrading the deck's best-executed pattern.

3. No matching antipattern - underuse of retrieval. One audience elicitation in 82 minutes, on a six-stage model the audience is explicitly meant to apply. A retrieval prompt at each stage boundary would cost seconds and is the cheapest available fix; every callback in the talk is a restatement instead. This narrowly avoids `nodding-room` only because that single elicitation exists.

4. No matching antipattern - `coda` absent. Twelve-plus books and resources are cited across the body, none collected at the end; everything defers to speaking.jbaru.ch. A one-slide reference coda would cost nothing and would serve the many attendees who will never open the shownotes.

5. No matching antipattern - the fake ending misfires positionally. The 'THE END.' slide (86) followed by Michael Scott's 'No, don't like that' (87) is a genuinely good gag, but it lands 25 slides before the real close and primes the room to disengage immediately before the objection-handling section - the talk's most directly actionable material.

6. No matching antipattern - zero speaker notes across 111 slides. The published deck carries no document layer, so a slide like the AMP+Fear matrix (a hand-drawn grid whose meaning is entirely verbal) is unreadable to anyone who downloads it.

7. Not a talk defect, recorded for the corpus: the auto-captions corrupt the two proper nouns the talk is built on ('coin Bradford', 'Butner'). Any future keyword search of this transcript for 'Cohen-Bradford' or 'BATNA' will return nothing.
