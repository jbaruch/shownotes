Cold visual gag before any content: both presenters walk on in FBI windbreakers (AI-generated onto a real photo, confirmed in the outro stats — 'FBI windbreaker generated by Gemini for the slides'). First captured words are the wordplay that carries the whole talk: 'agents here, agents on the stage. So, it's going to be plenty of different agentic stuff going on in this presentation.' Slide 2 is the Die Hard callout 'Agent Johnson. And Special Agent Johnson.' with the block quote 'No relation.' and the callout bar 'A LOT of agents in this room — all claiming to be the real one.' Bio lands on slide 3 ($ whoami) — not a delayed self-introduction, but the gag precedes it.

DIM 2 (Narrative). Discovery-demo arc, explicitly laddered. Slide 5 'Pick a Side' announces the frame: Team Baruch / Claude Code vs Team Viktor / GitHub Copilot, '5 stages. Same task. Same hardware. Raise hands. Who wins?' Then Stage 01 Face Detection, Stage 02 Who Is This, Stage 03 How Sure Is The Machine (first engineered failure), Stage 04 Delegation Day (regression), close. The 2024 baseline is the recurring measuring stick — 'what took an hour and a half in 2024 took less than half a minute in 2026' — and the close lands on 'No regression to 2024.' Note an internal inconsistency: slide 5 promises 5 stages, the outro stats say 'Six demo stages,' and the deck only ships four numbered Stage dividers.

DIM 3 (Humor). Dense two-hander banter, mostly situational rather than scripted. Best beats: the inverse Mr. Fusion (a cross-corpus signature bit — 'Mr. Fusion takes trash and turns it into energy. DIM 4 (Audience interaction). Very high — 15 distinct solicitations, and the room actually answers several. Same move on the sub-agent regression: 'anyone knows what happened?' — the room supplies 'Context', 'Start your session', 'if you hit the dumb zone', and the speaker explicitly banks the miss: 'that's a good assumption. The answer is actually — and I'm happy that no one knew.' Show-of-hands calibration throughout ('how many of you here actually like hardcore terminal user'; 'who uses skills today'; 'who of you follow the drama unfolding in Anthropic's issue tracker').

## Areas for Improvement (Dimension 14)

1. `going-meta` (severity: warning) — at least four separate behind-the-scenes interruptions land in the room: no conference wifi at the first prompt ('the cloud code needs to go to internet — and we don't have internet'), an unreadable IDE font that the audience had to flag twice before it was fixed ('that's too small, I can barely see it' / 'Leon is on the back on his phone means he cannot see anything'), the terminal-slide reveal ('how many of you can tell this is actually a terminal slide? — No one can tell. No one cares.'), and 'why did we prepare slides? It's very uncommon for us.' The failure narration itself ('congratulations people, we failed for the first time today') is the talk's thesis and should not be counted against it — but the equipment beats are pure friction.

2. `hiccup-words` (severity: warning, RECURRING) — filler density is high from both speakers throughout; 'um', 'uh', 'kind of like', 'and stuff', and Gamov's 'and what's not' recur constantly. Representative: 'so um in the demo we're going to be walk you through a process of building some application. Um because we is it on?'

3. `alienating-artifact` (severity: info) — two references visibly failed and were self-diagnosed on stage: the 'et voila' bit ('it's too niche, way too niche, maybe there is one person in the room that can get it and he's on the phone') and the 'skilling skills' escalation ('Tough crowd. Or maybe again showing my age'). The Princess Bride quote was also misquoted and corrected live.

4. Slide/demo desync (no matching antipattern). Repeated scrambles to find the right slide — 'Slides. Uh, slides. Ah, slides. Sorry. Slides. Slides. Where are slides?', 'that's the wrong place', 'go back go back go back' — and the Stage-04 divider was reached before its content ('I just noticed — that's a cool title'). With only 17 slides across 68 minutes, the deck should be trivially navigable; it wasn't.

5. Demo-surface legibility (no matching antipattern; `ant-fonts` covers slides only). The audience had to tell the presenters their editor text was unreadable, twice, before it was corrected. In a talk where the demo screen is on for ~85% of the runtime, the demo surface deserves the same legibility discipline as the deck.

6. Slide underuse, not overuse. Slides 10 ('The Silent Failures' table) and 11 ('Four Silent Bugs') are the deck's only analytical artifacts and both arrive after the same content was already delivered verbally, so they restate rather than reveal. The four-bug taxonomy would carry far more if the table appeared as the diagnosis rather than the recap.

NOT observed and worth recording: `shortchanged` is absent. Time discipline held ('We're on time. We still have plenty. Half an hour'), no sections were skipped and the close ran at full length — the speaker's most persistent corpus-wide issue did not fire here.
