## Rhetoric Notes (Dimensions 1-13)

SOURCE BOUNDARY. This talk has no video_url, no youtube_id and no transcript in the vault, so every observation below comes from one source: the complete authored 13-page PDF at slides/1FNzYNdUPZPNOQ7Q2HyZiGDY-BjJBqDax.pdf, inspected page by page. Dimensions that live in the spoken track (humor delivery, audience interaction, pacing, verbal signatures, transitions) can be described only as far as the pages carry them. Delivery language is deliberately left unset: the deck is entirely English, but slide language is not proof of spoken language and no transcript exists to settle it.

DIMENSION 1 - OPENING. The deck opens on a hook, not a person. Page 1 is the talk title inside a hand-drawn comic speech bubble; page 2 is the subtitle 'How I Learned to Stop Worrying and Love the AI' inside a jagged starburst. Neither carries a name, a photo or an employer. The bio does not arrive until page 3. That is a two-page deferral of the credential claim behind content, which is the delayed-self-introduction move in its deck-visible form. The subtitle is a Dr. Strangelove riff, so the opening also does cultural work: it frames AI anxiety as something to be laughed at rather than argued with, before a single technical claim is made.

DIMENSION 2 - NARRATIVE STRUCTURE. The page order carries a legible three-act skeleton. Act one is identity and logistics (1-5). Act two is a framing device: page 6 poses exactly three questions to an LLM, page 7 shows the answers 'summarized'. Act three works the third question out into mechanism (page 8, five build techniques), then into action tiers (page 9 free, page 10 paid), then into the payoff the title promised (page 11, ChatGPT plugins and embeddings), and resolves into a concrete ask (page 12). The DIY-with-ChatGPT throughline is planted on page 1 and paid off on page 11, which is a real arc rather than a list of topics. The weakness is proportion: the three-question frame promises equal treatment and delivers it only to the third question. Section boundaries are signalled by nothing but a background hue change; there are no divider pages, no agenda, no progress indicator.

DIMENSION 3 - HUMOR. Visible humor devices: the Strangelove title riff (page 2), the speaker's top-hat avatar dropped onto a cross-cultural chart (page 4), a rueful emoji stamped on the model's knowledge-cutoff quote (page 11), the phrase 'Fight evil flaky tests!' (page 8), 'Win Prizes (a.k.a. speed challenge)' (page 9), and a smiley in the closing speech bubble (page 13). The humor is dry and self-aware and it is placed on the pages rather than left to improvisation. Whether it landed is not knowable here.

DIMENSION 4 - AUDIENCE INTERACTION. The deck contains no poll page, no show-of-hands prompt, no question posed to the room. Page 6's three questions are addressed to a language model, not to the audience. Nothing in a flattened deck can establish what the speaker asked aloud, so no count is asserted.

DIMENSION 5 - TRANSITIONS. The only deck-visible transition device is background colour: the halftone field changes hue on nearly every page (yellow, yellow, teal, grey, blue, green, purple, red, olive, teal, purple, yellow, purple). It reads as rotation rather than semantics - no hue maps consistently to a content type - so it marks that something changed without saying what.

DIMENSION 6 - CLOSING. Page 12 is a four-item action list plus a QR code, a hand-drawn 'Scan me' arrow and the shownotes URL in large type. Page 13 is Q&A plus social handles. The close is a summary call to action with a resource list attached, and it is the strongest-designed content page in the deck.

DIMENSION 8 - SLIDE-TO-SPEECH RELATIONSHIP. This is the deck's main tension. Nine of thirteen pages are title-plus-bullet-list, and five of those carry four or more bullets. Pages 8 and 9 each compress five distinct engineering techniques into five one-line bullets. Those pages are not springboards - they are the argument, written out - which means the room can read ahead of the speaker. Set against that, page 10 carries a single bullet on an otherwise empty card and page 4 carries no bullets at all, so the density is uneven rather than uniformly heavy.

DIMENSION 9 - PERSUASION. The persuasive spine is authority-by-demonstration: ask the model, show its answers, then show what the model cannot know and how to fix it. Page 11 is the sharpest move - it raises the obvious objection to its own recommendation (the September 2021 cutoff, quoted verbatim) and answers it in the same block. What the deck conspicuously lacks is data: there is not a single benchmark, percentage or measured build-time figure anywhere in a talk about developer productivity. Page 10's entire argument for paid tooling is 'Gradle Enterprise (or similar)'.

DIMENSION 10 - CULTURAL REFERENCES. Two: the Strangelove subtitle, and the cross-cultural communication chart on page 4 annotated with a red ellipse around the Israel/Russia cluster and the speaker's top-hat avatar inside it. The chart is reproduced without a visible source credit.

DIMENSION 11 - TECHNICAL DELIVERY. No code appears anywhere in the deck. Complexity is handled by naming techniques rather than showing them, so the pages carry no scaffolding for a demo, a listing or a diagram walk-through.

DIMENSION 12 - PACING. Thirteen pages is a small budget for a conference slot, and the per-page load is lopsided: five techniques on page 8, one product name on page 10. Beyond that, pacing is a delivery property and this deck cannot report it.

DIMENSION 13 - SLIDE DESIGN. A genuinely coherent system, executed consistently. Two type families only: Bangers (hand-lettered comic display) for every title, Sniglet (rounded geometric sans) for every body line, with Apple Color Emoji for the two emoji. Bullet glyph is the multiplication sign. Every content page is a white panel with a heavy hand-drawn black border, rotated a few degrees off-square, floating on a halftone dot field with a soft drop shadow. Comic shapes are reserved for the loudest moments: a tailed speech bubble on pages 1 and 13, a starburst on page 2. A four-element footer (@jbaruch, event hashtag, #DPE, speaking.jbaru.ch) sits outside the panel on pages 5-12 in white Bangers, never crowding content. Images are few and specific: two of the speaker's own book covers inset on the bio page, the annotated chart, a photographed red marquee arrow pointing at the shownotes, and a QR code. Nothing is stock, nothing is pixelated, nothing is clip art. The one real craft defect is stale event identity: page 12's footer reads @devopsdaystlv and page 13 lists #shiftyourworld #infobipshift, both left over from earlier deliveries of this deck, while pages 5-11 correctly read #devoxx.

## Areas for Improvement (Dimension 14)

1. Bullet density (antipattern bullet-riddled-corpse, moderate). Nine of thirteen pages are bulleted lists and five carry four or more items. Pages 8 and 9 put five engineering techniques on one page each; the audience finishes reading before the speaker finishes the first one. Splitting page 8 into five one-idea pages, or replacing the list with a single build-timeline visual, would move the argument off the screen and back into the narration.

2. Orphan bullet on page 10 (antipattern injured-outlines, moderate). 'What your company should pay for' followed by exactly one item, 'Gradle Enterprise (or similar)', is the canonical single-child hierarchy. Either the heading should be the statement or the list should earn its second item - a cost frame, an evaluation criterion, a competitor.

3. Page 4's placement (antipattern alienating-artifact, weak). Circling Israel and Russia together and dropping the speaker's avatar inside that circle is a self-deprecating joke in most rooms. At Devoxx Ukraine in September 2023 it is a coin-flip. The chart is also uncredited, which is a separate and easily fixed problem.

4. No numbers. A developer-productivity talk with zero measured evidence - no build times, no cache hit rates, no percentage saved - argues entirely from assertion. One before/after figure would do more persuasive work than pages 8 through 10 combined.

5. Stale event stamps. The footer on page 12 and the hashtags on page 13 still name previous conferences. Missed rhetorical opportunity rather than antipattern, but it is the one detail that tells the room the deck was not made for them.

6. Structural signposting. There are no section dividers, no agenda and no progress cue; hue rotation is the only boundary marker, and it does not map to meaning. In a thirteen-page deck this is survivable, but the three-question frame on page 6 is a ready-made structure the deck never revisits.
