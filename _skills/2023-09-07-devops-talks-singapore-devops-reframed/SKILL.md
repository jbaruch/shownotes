## Rhetoric Notes (Dimensions 1-13)

Dimension 1 - Opening. The talk opens on the room rather than the topic. The first spoken line names the slot itself ("uh you made it last session of the last day and look at you you'll look okay let's try to wake you up a little bit") and then promises a conspiracy theory. Rendered page 1 is the title inside a tilted comic panel, page 2 is the hook: a conspiracy-theory grading pyramid credited on the page to Abbie Richards, with the spoken line naming the same axis the chart draws. The hook frames the thesis as a claim that has to be proved rather than asserted, and the handoff into the argument is clean rather than a dangling gag.

Dimension 2 - Narrative structure. Problem, diagnosis, solution, carried by one device. The thesis - DevOps is a plot of Ops people to make developers work harder - is argued through three explicitly numbered exhibits: the Phoenix Project cover walked character by character (page 6), a Google results page showing that every voice selling DevOps sells infrastructure (page 8), and the 2022 Accelerate report's own definition of work types (pages 22 to 26). The verdict lands, then is immediately steel-manned: the Ops pain is real and the methodology justified. Act two attacks the naming damage, act three names Developer Productivity Engineering as the developer-side implementation. Every abstraction arrives after an instance of it; the term "developer productivity engineering" is not spoken until the room has already seen the pain it names.

Dimension 3 - Humour. Load-bearing rather than decorative. The salary gag is the punchline of exhibit two, not a break from it, and the reaction-image pages sit on the argument's hinges. The recurring self-undercut is aimed at the speaker's own commercial interest twice: his employer's products are introduced as "awesome t-shirts also very nice tools", and his two co-authored books are demolished in the same sentence that names them.

Dimension 4 - Audience interaction. Eleven distinct solicitations, counting only moments where the speaker turns to the room and asks for a response - a question, a show of hands, or a direct call-out - and not addresses or instructions. Three of the eleven produce answers the transcript records: a named volunteer is put on the spot for a build-time number and supplies "two minutes"; a follow-up confirms he runs that build himself; a third asks what the room does during those two minutes and waits for "sometimes both". Those recorded productions are what make the moves commitments rather than self-answered questions.

Dimension 5 - Transitions. Verbal bridges are explicit and often comic: "but Jokes Aside", "exhibit number two", "and Exhibit C", "next thing you know". Visually a boundary is signalled by switching the background colour field and dropping in a single-declaration page - a starburst on pages 33 and 41, a speech bubble on page 38, a full-field type page on page 37.

Dimension 6 - Closing. Three closing jobs on three separate pages: a recap of the argument's conclusions (page 58), four concrete and immediately executable asks with a scannable code (page 59), and a thanks page with handles (page 60). The last spoken beat is local - the book raffle happening in the room right now.

Dimension 7 - Verbal signatures. "Is not a thing" is the talk's spine and appears both spoken and set inside the starburst on page 33. "It's fine" recurs as a concession marker. "Next thing you know" carries three separate escalations.

Dimension 8 - Slide-to-speech. Judged from the rendered pages. The deck carries considerably more than the narration does, and the excess is deliberate: screenshots, tables and the conspiracy chart hold detail the speaker never reads, with the shownotes destination named aloud and repeated in the footer of nearly every page. Only four of the sixty rendered pages are genuinely wordless (19, 27, 29, 57). The rest of the image-led pages carry baked-in display lettering that states the claim, which is a different move from image-plus-narration.

Dimensions 10 and 11 - References and technical delivery. Western internet-meme vocabulary throughout: Scooby-Doo unmasking (twice, pages 9 and 46, the second reusing the template with new labels), Futurama, Tom and Jerry, Dr Evil, a Botero-style baby, an It's Always Sunny corkboard, the SILO poster used as a silo pun. Complexity is handled by analogy and by one recurring diagram rather than by code; all sixty pages were inspected and none carries a code listing of any length.

Dimension 12 - Pacing. About thirty-eight minutes for sixty rendered pages (registered source duration 2267 s, 5,024 transcript words, roughly 133 words per minute), with no time-pressure language anywhere in the transcript and a complete, unhurried three-part close. The seven DPE pillars in the final third get less room per idea than the three exhibits did.

Dimension 13 - Slide design. One comic vocabulary holds across the whole deck: a halftone dot field over every background including screenshots, tilted white panels with heavy inked outlines and drop shadows, speech bubbles with tails on thirteen pages, starburst declaration panels on two, a hand-lettered display face for headlines over a rounded sans for body. The structural device is a three-circle diagram that recurs on pages 47, 48, 49, 50 and 53 with its labels rewritten at each act boundary - Dev/Ops/QA, then People/Process/Tools, then the same three qualified as Productive, then a deliberate hole marked "?!?!" that page 53 fills with Developer Productivity Engineering. That hole and its answer are the deck's payoff.

## Areas for Improvement (Dimension 14)

Ant fonts, moderate. Three pages carry text the back row cannot read while the argument depends on it. The ChatGPT answer on page 45 is small low-contrast grey on a dark tilted panel and is read aloud as the load-bearing definition of platform engineering. The borrowed percentages table on page 23 is set small and its bottom row is clipped by the footer strip. The survey bar chart on page 52 puts its three question labels far below room size, and its title is clipped at the top edge.

Floodmarks, moderate. The four-element branding strip - personal handle, conference handle, hashtag, shownotes URL - sits on fifty-three of the sixty pages rather than on the bookends. It is thin and cheap, but it is persistent, and on at least four pages it overlaps the content underneath it: the salary figures on pages 12 and 13, the clipped bottom row of the table on page 23, and the source line on the vendor slide on page 55.

Going meta, moderate. Four beats are about the talk rather than in it: the opening aside that the talk was swapped that morning after another speaker's keynote, the aside about whether the recording buttons were pressed, a pointer to a different talk of his own on cognitive load, and the remark that a 2023 conference talk cannot avoid ChatGPT. Each is small; together they spend about a minute of a thirty-eight-minute slot.

Two talk-local suggestions beyond the antipatterns. The seven DPE pillars arrive as a single page of seven coloured boxes (page 54) and are then narrated straight through in under two minutes, which is the thinnest stretch of an otherwise well-paced talk; two or three pillars given the exhibit treatment would land better than seven listed. And the borrowed vendor and report pages break the comic vocabulary hard - pages 22, 23, 51, 52, 55 and 56 arrive as corporate collateral with at most a hand-lettered title bolted on, rather than the data being redrawn in the deck's own language, so the strongest evidence in the talk is delivered in its least persuasive visual register.
